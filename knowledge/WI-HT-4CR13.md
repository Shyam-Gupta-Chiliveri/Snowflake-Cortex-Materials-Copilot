# Hardening and tempering of 4Cr13 / X46CrS13 axles

## 1. Scope
Applies to martensitic stainless steel axles in 4Cr13 and the free-machining variant X46CrS13, nominal diameter 8 mm (7.8 mm after the diameter change). The goal is a hardness that resists wear while keeping enough ductility to survive the bending test without brittle fracture.

## 2. Hardening
Austenitise at 1045 °C ± 5 °C, then quench. As-quenched martensite is very hard but brittle: in the test data, untempered groups (Version 6, Groups A and B) reach the highest mean HV10 and the lowest ductility.

## 3. Tempering routes in use
- Additional tempering after hardening: 180, 200, 220 or 240 °C for 1 h (Version 6, Groups C to F).
- Series tempering at 200 °C for 3 h, optionally followed by 220 °C for 1 h (Version 11, Groups H and I).
- Series tempering with the temperature not recorded (Version 3, Group B, Freund press).
- Vacuum hardening with 230 °C, optionally re-tempered at 230 °C for 1 h (Version 14).

## 4. Effect of tempering temperature
Higher tempering temperature in the 180 to 240 °C window lowers hardness and raises ductility and bending force. Tempering relieves quench stresses and lets carbon leave the supersaturated martensite. Do not temper in the 400 to 550 °C range: chromium carbide precipitation there causes temper embrittlement and lower corrosion resistance.

## 5. Release criteria for this demo
A route is recommended for series production when mean ductility is at least 30 %, mean HV10 is at least 600, and the share of samples with ductility below 23 % is under 5 %. These limits are demo acceptance criteria set for this project, not values from a standard.

## 6. Reaction plan
If a batch shows ductility below 23 % or cleavage-dominated fracture in SEM, hold the batch, check the furnace record (temperature and time), and re-temper or scrap after engineering review. Record the event as a nonconformance and run an 8D if it reached a customer.
