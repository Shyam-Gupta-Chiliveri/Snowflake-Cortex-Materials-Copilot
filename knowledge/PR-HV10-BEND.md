# Hardness and bending tests for axles

## 1. Vickers hardness HV10
Hardness is measured with a Vickers indenter at a test force of 98.07 N (HV10), following the Vickers method described in ISO 6507-1. Each sample is tested several times; the record keeps the mean, minimum and maximum HV10. A large spread between minimum and maximum points to inhomogeneous hardening or decarburisation.

## 2. Number of samples
A test lot uses 5 samples as standard. Lots with 3 samples are allowed for trial runs; their statistics are less reliable and should not be used alone for a release decision.

## 3. Three-point bending
The axle is loaded in three-point bending until fracture. The record keeps the average, minimum and maximum bending force in newtons. Higher bending force at fracture together with higher ductility means the material deforms before it breaks.

## 4. Ductility and brittleness percentage
Each test also reports a ductility percentage and its complement, the brittleness percentage (they add up to 100). Low ductility together with high hardness is the typical signature of under-tempered martensite.

## 5. Data quality rules
Reject a record when HV10 minimum is above the mean or the maximum is below the mean, or when ductility and brittleness do not add up to 100 within 0.1. The pipeline counts such records in the data-quality checks.
